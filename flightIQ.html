FlightIQ

An AI-powered flight congestion intelligence dashboard built on a simple premise: most people optimize for price, but strategic thinkers optimize for outcome.

The idea

Every traveler who books the cheapest flight without checking congestion patterns is making a reactive choice — optimizing one variable and absorbing the cost elsewhere: delays, stress, missed connections. FlightIQ reframes the decision around outcome rather than price alone, surfacing the signals that actually predict a smooth trip.

On the surface it's a dashboard. Underneath, it's an exercise in reading the environment before committing — the same instinct that separates reactive decisions from strategic ones.

What it surfaces

01

Ground stop programs

Live FAA ground-stop data — the signal that changes a decision but rarely shows up in a normal flight search.

02

Airport congestion scores

A composite view of how congested an airport is likely to be, beyond just on-time percentages.

03

7-day forecasts

Forward-looking congestion trends so a decision can be made ahead of the travel date, not the morning of.

04

Best departure windows

The three best windows to fly, ranked by total travel time rather than fare alone.

How it's built

  • NetworkX-based graph optimization runs three independent strategies — cost, time, and congestion — because the "fastest" route and the "best" route are almost never the same thing.
  • Single-metric thinking (cheapest fare, shortest duration) consistently misses options that multi-strategy optimization surfaces.
  • Resilience is designed into the architecture, not bolted on: when live APIs are unavailable, the system falls back to BTS statistical models, and historical patterns fill gaps in real-time congestion data.
NetworkX graph optimization multi-strategy routing (cost / time / congestion) FAA ground-stop data BTS statistical fallback models

The bigger takeaway

Every domain has a congestion problem — bottlenecks that aren't visible from the surface, windows of opportunity that close fast, and paths that look obvious but cost more than expected. Building FlightIQ was as much a lesson in strategic thinking as it was a technical project: the leaders and systems that consistently outperform aren't luckier, they've built better instruments for reading the environment.
